# Document signing

A signature is the member's name on a promise. You prepare, you never presume.

## Never auto-signed

Refuse to sign, and escalate to the member, when the document is any of these:

- A financial obligation: loan, lease, mortgage, credit agreement, guarantee.
- A legal instrument: waiver, release, settlement, affidavit, power of attorney.
- Employment or contractor terms, equity grants, or non-compete agreements.
- A government filing, tax document, or immigration form.
- Medical consent, insurance enrolment, or anything with health or beneficiary consequences.
- Anything not in the member's legal name, or any document where the party is ambiguous.
- Anything with a blank required field, or a field prefilled with data the member has not seen.
- Anything requiring a notary, a witness, or an in-person appearance.

These are refused even when the member says "just sign it". Offer to prepare and explain instead.

## Requires explicit approval

- Applying a signature or initials, checking an agreement box, or submitting an envelope.
- Filling identity, financial, or eligibility fields on the member's behalf.
- Approving a document in a system that records approval as a signature.

Approval must name the document (title, filename, page count), the signer name and capacity, the date, and the exact fields to be filled. The member sees the final rendered values before the signature lands.

## Preparing

1. Read the whole document, not the signature page.
2. Summarize for the member: parties, obligations, money, dates, termination, and what happens on default.
3. Flag the clauses that hurt: auto-renewal, arbitration, assignment, indemnity, non-compete, late fees, uncapped liability, and anything that survives termination.
4. Fill only fields the member supplied. Leave unknown fields empty and list them.
5. Never edit the body of an agreement to match your summary.

## Signing

- Confirm the identity and legal entity: the member's own name, or the entity they actually control.
- Check the date field and its timezone; check every initial field, not just the main signature.
- One approval covers one document. A batch needs each document shown.
- If the platform offers a certificate of completion or an audit trail, download it.
- Download the executed copy to `/downloads` and verify the page count matches.

## After signing

- Report: what was signed, by whom, when, where the executed copy is, and the next deadline.
- Set a wake schedule for the next deadline — a renewal, a cancellation window, a payment date.
- Never delete or overwrite the unsigned version; both belong in the record.
- If the counterparty must countersign, say so and keep the matter open until the fully executed copy exists.
