# Email permissions

The rules behind the four questions. Read this before any send that is not a one-line reply inside an existing grant.

## Certified scope template

Write the grant down in this shape, in the handoff report:

- identity: the mailbox and the display name the recipient will see
- recipients: exact addresses, with cc and bcc listed separately
- purpose: the outcome the mail serves
- window: the deadline for the send, and the expiry of the grant
- may assert: the facts the member supplied or that evidence supports
- may not assert: commitments, prices, dates, or opinions that were not given
- attachments: named files, or none

A grant without a window expires when the thread goes quiet for seven days.

## Exact-text approval mechanics

1. Send the block as one message. No commentary inside it.
2. Label it clearly: subject, from, to, cc, body, attachments.
3. Ask one question: approve as shown, or change what.
4. Ship the approved bytes. If any tool re-renders the text (quoted history, signature, template), compare the final rendered message before sending.

## Provenance rules

- A claim is either quoted from the member, read from evidence with a path, or cited from a returned URL. Anything else is an inference, and inferences do not commit the member.
- Never attribute a position to the member that they did not take.
- Never confirm a meeting, a payment, a delivery date, or a legal position the member has not seen.
- If you must send something unverified, name it in the report as unverified and ask the parent before sending.

## Never sendable by email

- Vault values in plaintext: passwords, card numbers, bank details, government ids.
- One-time verification codes.
- Confidential material belonging to someone else: another member's data, private thread contents, internal records.
- Legal, medical, or financial commitments, and anything that reads as advice on those topics.
- Bulk or unsolicited mail, and anything that would look like spam from the member's domain.
- Attachments you have not opened and verified.

## Escalation ladder

1. Covered by the grant and the text is approved: send.
2. Covered by the grant, but the final text is new: get exact-text approval.
3. Outside the grant, related to its purpose: ask the parent for an extension, naming the recipient and the sentence in question.
4. Outside the grant and unrelated: decline the send and report. Do not stretch the purpose to fit.
