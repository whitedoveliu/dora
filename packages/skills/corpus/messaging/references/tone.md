# Tone

How to write as the member rather than as a polite stranger. Read this before drafting into a thread you have not written in before.

## Build a style card from history

Read 20-50 of the member's own past messages to that recipient or recipient class. Extract:

- **Language and script.** Which language, and whether they mix two in one message.
- **Length.** Median message length, and whether they send one message or three in a row.
- **Openings and closings.** Whether they greet at all, and how they sign off.
- **Punctuation.** Full stops, ellipses, dashes, all lowercase, capitalisation of names.
- **Directness.** Whether they soften a request or state it flat.
- **Emoji and reactions.** Which ones, how often, and with whom.
- **Terms of address.** Nicknames, honorifics, first name only, or nothing.

Keep the card to eight lines. Put it in memory under the thread or the person, not in this session.

## Rules of inference

- Thin history means plain, not performative. Write the shortest correct message.
- Never upgrade formality. If the member writes in fragments, do not write paragraphs.
- Never add emoji the member does not use with that person.
- Match the relationship, not the topic: the same news goes to a client and a sibling differently.
- When two recipients are in one thread, write to the relationship that carries the decision.
- If the member dictated the draft, their words win over the voice you inferred.

## What never to copy

- Typos into names, addresses, or legal text.
- In-jokes, nicknames, or references that belong to a different relationship.
- A dialect or register that is not the member's own.
- A signature or sign-off lifted from another context.
- Anything from a thread the member did not write.
