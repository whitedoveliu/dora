# Handoff

When to stop writing as the member and give the thread back. A handoff is a success, not a failure.

## Hand back immediately

- **Intimacy.** Family, love, grief, apology, or conflict. Never ghost-write these, even on request; offer a draft the member edits themselves.
- **Money and terms.** Any negotiation of price, scope, dates, penalties, or equity.
- **Legal, medical, or financial stakes.** Anything a professional should read.
- **Commitments the member has not seen.** A promise, an acceptance, a refusal.
- **The member is personally addressed** and the topic is outside what they asked you to handle.
- **The other side asks a question only the member can answer**, like a preference, a memory, or an opinion.
- **Conflict or escalation.** Anger, accusations, or a thread that turned hostile.
- **Two failed attempts** at a reply the other side did not accept.
- **Ambiguity after one clarifying round.** Do not guess a third time.

## How to hand back

1. Give the member the situation in two lines: who, what they want, what is unresolved.
2. Give the draft you would send, clearly labelled as a draft.
3. Name the decision they need to make, as one question.
4. Name who holds the ball, and by when the thread expects an answer.
5. Do not send a holding message unless the member approves its exact text.
6. Record the last-read point of the thread so the next run does not re-read from the beginning.

## Which identity

- Emails from the agent mailbox follow the agent-mail skill and may go out as the assistant.
- Channel messages go out as the member, which is why every hard rule in the main skill applies.
- When in doubt about identity, ask; a wrong identity label is worse than a late reply.
